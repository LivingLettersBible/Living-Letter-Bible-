Choosing a Translation

There's no single “correct” English translation — each makes different, defensible choices about how closely to mirror the original wording versus the original meaning.

Word-for-word translations (ESV, NASB, KJV) stay close to the structure of the Hebrew and Greek. They reward slow, careful reading and are well suited to study.

Thought-for-thought translations (NIV, NLT, CSB) prioritize clarity and natural English phrasing. They're often easier for daily reading, especially aloud or for newer readers.

Our honest advice: pick one, stick with it for a season, and don't let translation-shopping become a reason to stall. Any of the above will serve a daily reading plan well.

Five Questions for Any Passage

Keep these nearby while you read. You don't need to answer all five every day — pick whichever opens the text for you.

Observation

What does it actually say?

Before interpreting, just notice. Who is speaking? To whom? What happens, in order?

Context

What surrounds it?

What comes just before and after? Who first read this, and what were they facing?

Meaning

What is it saying about God?

Every passage reveals something about who God is or how He acts. Name it plainly.

Honesty

What's my honest reaction?

Comfort, resistance, confusion, conviction — write down what's actually true, not what sounds right.

Response

What does this ask of me today?

Not someday — today. One concrete, small, doable response is worth more than a vague resolution.
