Faithful in Small Things

Sep 1, 2026 · 4 min read

← Back to Devotionals

“One who is faithful in a very little is also faithful in much, and one who is dishonest in a very little is also dishonest in much.” — Luke 16:10

There's a version of the spiritual life we all quietly wait for: the dramatic one. The call to ministry that arrives like a lightning bolt. The mission trip that reorders your priorities overnight. The public act of courage that costs you something and proves, once and for all, that your faith is real.

Most of us are still waiting. And most of us will keep waiting, because that is mostly not how it works.

Jesus' words in Luke 16 aren't really about money, even though that's the immediate context. They're about scale. He's telling His disciples that the small, unwatched moments — how you handle a little bit of money, a little bit of trust, a little bit of attention — are not a lesser category of faithfulness. They are the training ground for it, and often the whole of it.

The room nobody sees

Think about your actual week. The apology you did or didn't offer after snapping at someone you love. The five extra minutes you gave to a conversation you wanted to end. The honest answer you gave when a vague one would have been easier. The Tuesday you opened your Bible for six minutes before the day swallowed you whole.

None of that will ever be a testimony. No one is going to ask you to share it at church. And that is exactly why it matters — because faithfulness that only shows up when someone's watching isn't faithfulness, it's performance.

Living Letters exists partly because of this conviction: the daily, unglamorous act of sitting with Scripture for a few minutes is not a warm-up for the real spiritual life. It is the real spiritual life, most days, for most people. The letter God is writing on your heart is being written in ordinary ink, in ordinary rooms, mostly when no one is looking.

A small thing to try

If today feels unremarkable, that's not a sign you're behind. Open to wherever you are in a reading plan. Read slowly. Notice one true thing. Let that be enough for today — not because more wouldn't be good, but because faithfulness in a very little is never actually little to the One writing the letter.
