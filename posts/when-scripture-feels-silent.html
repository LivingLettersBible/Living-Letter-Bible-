When Scripture Feels Silent

Aug 25, 2026 · 5 min read

← Back to Devotionals

“How long, O Lord? Will you forget me forever?” — Psalm 13:1

Sooner or later, it happens to almost everyone who reads the Bible with any regularity: you open to the day's passage, you read the words, and… nothing. No conviction, no comfort, no sense of a voice behind the text. Just ink on a page, and a faint, uncomfortable question underneath it — is this even working?

If that's where you are right now, you're in good company. Nearly a third of the Psalms are complaints about exactly this kind of silence. David doesn't perform his way past it or paper over it with forced cheerfulness. He names it, directly, to God's face: how long?

Silence is not the same as absence

It's worth separating two things that feel identical in the moment but aren't: God's presence and your sense of it. Scripture never promises that reading the Bible will produce a feeling on command. It promises something slower and less visible — that the word does not return void, that it is doing work whether or not you can feel it happening (Isaiah 55:10–11).

A seed doesn't feel itself growing underground. That doesn't mean nothing is happening.

What to actually do

First, keep the appointment even when it feels empty. Skipping the dry seasons is the surest way to make them permanent. Second, pray honestly about the dryness itself — tell God plainly that the page feels flat, the way David did. That's not a failure of faith; in the Psalms, it is faith. Third, lower the bar for what counts as success. Some days, finishing the reading is the whole task. You don't have to feel something to have been faithful.

And if the silence stretches for weeks, it's worth asking whether something else needs attention — sleep, grief, an unresolved conflict, an unacknowledged doubt. Sometimes the page is quiet because we're not; sometimes it's quiet for no discoverable reason at all, and that's allowed too.

Either way, you're not the first letter that's gone a while without feeling read. Stay at the table. The Author isn't finished.
