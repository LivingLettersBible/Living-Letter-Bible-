The Ink and the Heart

Aug 18, 2026 · 4 min read

← Back to Devotionals

“You show that you are a letter from Christ, the result of our ministry, written not with ink but with the Spirit of the living God, not on tablets of stone but on tablets of human hearts.” — 2 Corinthians 3:3

The church in Corinth had a habit of asking for credentials. Some visiting teachers had shown up with letters of recommendation — official documents vouching for their authority — and it seems the Corinthians started wondering whether Paul had any of his own.

His answer is strange, and it's the reason this whole project is named after it: you are his letter of recommendation. Not a document he carries, but a people he has watched change. Written not with ink, but with the Spirit. Not on a scroll, but on a heart.

A letter is not a list

It's worth sitting with the image itself. A letter isn't a rulebook or a record of transactions. It's relational by nature — written by someone, to someone, meant to be read by someone else. When Paul calls the Corinthians a letter, he's saying their transformed lives are a kind of communication: evidence, visible to anyone watching, of a Spirit at work.

That reframes something we can easily get backwards. We tend to think of Bible reading as one-directional — we take something in. But 2 Corinthians 3 suggests a second, quieter movement running the other way: as we read, something is being written into us, slowly enough that we rarely feel the pen move.

What kind of letter are you becoming?

This is not a call to perform holiness for an audience. Paul is describing something that happens whether or not anyone is watching, the way ink actually changes paper rather than sitting on top of it. The question worth asking isn't does anyone see it, but is it actually happening — is the text you're reading being allowed to reach past your eyes and into the actual shape of your days?

That's the whole aim behind these reading plans and devotionals: not more information about the Bible, but time enough, and attention enough, for the Spirit to keep writing. You are already somebody's letter. The only question is what it says.
